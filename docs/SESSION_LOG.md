## Session 3 — 2026-09-26 — Notification survey and ingestion architecture decisions

The session began by tidying Git after pull request #3: switching to `main`, pulling, and deleting the merged branch locally and its stale remote reference. On the operator's explicit request, Bank A's notification emails were surveyed locally. A header-only inventory listed the message types, a few samples were stored as `.eml` files in the ignored `data/raw/` directory, and a full in-memory scan confirmed that every transaction notification matches one of three sentence patterns: credit-card purchase, account purchase, and ATM withdrawal. The scan also found two currency formats and no reversal notifications. A handful of legitimate messages failed SPF but passed DMARC, so authenticity checks will require DMARC. `TRANSACTION_CASES.md` and `THREAT_MODEL.md` now reflect these findings without naming the institution or quoting its wording. New decisions record: surveying formats before defining the transaction model; polling first, then Pub/Sub, then a webhook; local execution before any cloud hosting; PostgreSQL as the transactional database; pseudonymous institutions with a generic parser driven by local provider profiles; and keeping the repository public, with a private companion repository as a future option. Next session: Bank A's PDF account statements for backfill, reconciliation, and installment tracking.

## Session 4 — 2026-09-26 — Statement survey and profile-driven statement parser

On the operator's explicit request, Bank A's mailbox was inventoried again, this time for attachments. Four monthly statement types arrive as password-protected PDFs: checking account, credit card (one per card), credit line interest settlement, and consumer loan installment notice. Sample files were stored under the ignored `data/raw/statements/` directory with owner-only permissions. A local survey found that checking account statements are tables whose debit and credit columns differ only by position, while card statements list one entry per line in sections with stated totals, including installment details and, when there is foreign activity, a US-dollar part that keeps the original-currency amount. `pdfplumber` was added as the PDF dependency. A generic statement parser now offers two engines, column tables and line patterns, driven by an ignored local profile, and every parse produces reconciliation checks against stated balances and totals. All surveyed samples reconcile. Tests use a fictional profile, synthetic words, and a generated PDF. The findings, two decisions, threat model updates, and roadmap wording were documented, and PDFs were added to the ignore rules. Next: download every statement, validate the parser across the full history, and define the Stage 1 transaction model from both sources.

## Session 5 — 2026-09-29 — Full-history statement validation

On the operator's explicit request, every Bank A statement attached to the mailbox was downloaded to the ignored `data/raw/statements/` directory with owner-only permissions: about four years of checking account, credit card, credit line interest, and loan installment statements. The PDF password was kept in the operating system's keychain for the session instead of in a file or environment variable. The parser was run over every checking account and credit card statement, and all of them passed their reconciliation checks without profile changes. Temporary local scripts then chained consecutive statements. Checking account balances and periods were continuous, and entries on the shared boundary day were not duplicated. Card statements chained through a card replacement and skipped periods with nothing to bill. One card statement was never delivered by email; the operator will obtain it from the institution. The findings were documented, and a decision defers automated continuity checks to the Stage 1 transaction model, which is the next step.
